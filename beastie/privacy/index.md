# Beastie Odyssey: privacy policy

**Effective date:** 18 November 2026

Beastie Odyssey is a game for players of all ages, children included, made by
**chappitneep**. This page explains, in plain words, what happens to
information when you play it.

## The short version

- **We don't receive anything about you.** The game has no accounts, no
  sign-in, no analytics and no servers of ours. Nothing you do in the game is
  sent to us.
- **Your progress stays on your device.** It isn't sent anywhere or backed up.
- **The game asks your age once, when it first opens.** It keeps only
  "under 16" or "16 or over", never the age you typed.
- **Under 16: no ads at all.** Google's ad service is never started, and
  nothing is sent to Google for ads. Skipping a level is always free.
- **16 or over: an ad only when you ask for one.** If you're stuck on a level,
  you can choose to watch a short video to skip it. Only then does the game
  contact Google's ad service. Google's ad service does receive some
  information about the device when it does; this page says exactly what.
- **The ads are never personalised** and are always suitable for all ages.

## Exactly what is and isn't tracked

| What | Where it goes | Who can see it |
|---|---|---|
| Your progress and settings | A file on your device | Nobody. Not us, not Google |
| Your answer to the age question ("under 16" or "16 or over" only) | The same file on your device | Nobody. Not us, not Google |

**Never collected, by anyone, through the game:** your name, your age or
birthday, your precise location, contacts, photos, camera, microphone, what
you do in other apps, or how you play the game. A player under 16 sends
nothing to Google for ads, ever.

## The age question

The first time the game opens, before anything else, it asks **How old are
you?** There is no right answer: the game is the same game either way, and
whatever you answer, you can play all of it. Only the result is kept, on the
device: **under 16** or **16 or over**. If you close the question without
answering, the game treats you as under 16 and asks again next time.

The answer can't be changed in Settings. To answer again, clear the game's
storage in your device's settings, or uninstall and reinstall it. That also
deletes your progress. If you'd bought **No ads**, it comes back from Google
Play by itself.

## Ads

The game is free. It can show one kind of ad: a short video that a player
aged 16 or over can **choose** to watch to skip a level they've failed three
times. There are no other ads: none between levels, none during play, no
banners. A player under 16, a player who owns **No ads**, and anyone on a Lesson
(a level that teaches something new) skips for free instead.

**Nothing happens for ads until you press Watch.** The game doesn't start
Google's ad service when it opens, when you fail a level, or when the skip is
offered. Only when a player aged 16 or over presses **Watch** does it:

1. **Ask Google's consent tool.** This is Google's User Messaging Platform, a
   consent tool Google certifies. It asks Google's servers whether the law
   where you are needs your consent, and Google works that out from the
   device's connection. In the UK, the European Economic Area and
   Switzerland, it shows **Google's consent message**, which names Google and
   explains what it stores and why. Your answer is kept on the device by the
   consent tool. We never see it. This happens once each time the game is
   opened, at the first Watch.
2. **Ask for one ad**, under the rules below. While it loads, the game shows
   "Getting a video…". If no ad arrives within a few seconds, or none can be
   had (you're offline, or Google has none to show), the skip is free.

If the consent tool doesn't allow any ad (for example, the first time you
press Watch while offline), the game doesn't start the ad service at all, and
the skip is free.

**Every ad, for every player who sees one:**

- is **never personalised**: it isn't chosen from anything about you or what
  you do in other apps;
- is rated **G**, suitable for all ages;
- comes from **Google AdMob** alone. No other ad company is involved.

**If you refuse in Google's consent message**, or accept only some of it, the
ad is a **limited ad**. Google says a limited ad doesn't use anything stored
on the device to identify it, and doesn't use cookies or device storage to
check for fraud either: we've turned that option off. The ad itself may be
saved on the device for a short while so it can play. Limited ads still need
the device's IP address to deliver the ad. If no limited ad is available, the
skip is free.

**If you accept**, or if you're outside the UK, the European Economic Area and
Switzerland, the ad is an ordinary non-personalised ad. To show it, count it,
limit how often the same ad is shown and protect against fraud, Google's ad
service stores and reads a little information on the device, such as the app
set ID.

**What we tell Google, and what we don't.** We don't tag players who press
Watch as children, because only players who said they're 16 or over can
press it. The game doesn't have permission to read the advertising ID on
Android 13 and newer. On Android 12 and older, Android lets any app read it
without that permission, so Google's ad service may read it after you press
Watch. You can reset or delete the advertising ID in your device's settings.

**Changing your answer.** Once you have answered Google's consent message,
**Settings** shows **Privacy options**, from then on, without needing to
press Watch first. Showing the row sends nothing; tapping it asks Google's
consent tool for its privacy options and opens Google's consent message
again. Your new answer applies from the next ad. You can also stop ads altogether by buying **No
ads**.

**Google is responsible for what it does with that information.** It acts as
an independent controller: **Google LLC** for players in the UK, and
**Google Ireland Limited** for players in the European Economic Area and
Switzerland. See Google's privacy policy (<https://policies.google.com/privacy>)
and how Google uses information from apps that use its services
(<https://business.safety.google/privacy/>). Google says the information its
ad service collects is encrypted on its way to Google.

## Buying No ads

**No ads** is a one-time purchase that removes every ad and makes every skip
free. It is made through **Google Play**, which handles the payment, behind a
check meant to stop young children buying by accident. We never see payment
details, a name or an account.

Each time the game opens, it asks the Google Play app on the device whether
No ads has been bought on the Google account the device uses, so it comes
back on a new phone or after reinstalling. It confirms a new purchase, as Play
requires, and remembers the answer in its file on the device. Google Play's
privacy policy applies to purchases and to what Play itself knows.

## If you email us

If you write to us, we receive your email address and whatever you write.
Please don't send more than your question needs. We use it only to reply, and
never for a mailing list or marketing. We delete it once the matter is
closed, and within 6 months at the latest, unless we need it to deal with a
complaint. If it's clear the email is from a child, we delete it as soon as
we've replied.

## Why we're allowed to do this (lawful basis)

- **Ads, in the UK, the European Economic Area and Switzerland:** your
  consent, given in Google's consent message, which you can change at any
  time through **Settings → Privacy options**. If you refuse, the limited ad
  you asked for by pressing Watch is shown on the basis of our legitimate
  interest in funding the game, with nothing stored on the device to identify
  it.
- **The No ads purchase:** carrying out the purchase you asked for.
- **Emails:** our legitimate interest in answering you.

## Where information goes

Ad information is handled by Google, which may process it in the United
States. Google protects transfers from the UK, the European Economic Area and
Switzerland with the standard contractual clauses approved for that purpose.

## How long information is kept

- **The file on your device** (progress, settings, the age answer, No ads):
  until you clear the game's storage or uninstall it.
- **Google's consent tool's note of your answer:** on the device, until you
  change it, clear the game's storage or uninstall it.
- **Ad information Google receives:** Google decides how long it keeps it;
  see Google's privacy policy.
- **Emails:** as above.

## Children

The game is made to be suitable for children. A player who says they're under
16 gets no ads and no ad service at all, so nothing about them is sent to
Google for ads, and nothing about them ever reaches us. We don't ask for, and
never knowingly receive, personal information from children.

**For parents in the United States (COPPA).** The game asks every player's age
before anything else. A child under 13 who answers truthfully gets no ads and
no ad service: the game collects no personal information from them, and sends
none to Google or anyone else. The only information that could reach us is an
email someone chooses to send, which we delete as soon as we've replied.

If your child has said they're 16 or over, they could choose to watch an ad
to skip a level. Google's ad service then receives the information in the
table above. The ads are never personalised and always rated G, but the
request is not marked as coming from a child. To put this right, clear the
game's storage or reinstall it, and answer the age question again. You can
also buy No ads, which stops every ad. Uninstalling the game deletes
everything it keeps on the device, and you can contact us with any question.

## Your rights

You have the right to ask what information we hold about you, to have it
corrected or deleted, to object, and to withdraw consent. Because the game
sends us nothing, the only information we could hold is an email you've sent
us. For information Google holds, Google's privacy policy explains how to see
and delete it. You can change your ad consent in **Settings → Privacy
options**, when the game shows it.

**If you're unhappy** with how we've handled your information, please
complain to us first, at the address below. We'll acknowledge your complaint
within 30 days. You also have the right to complain to the UK's data
protection regulator, the Information Commission (formerly the ICO),
<https://ico.org.uk>, or to the data protection authority where you live.

## Changes to this policy

If the game starts handling information differently, we'll update this page
and its effective date before that version of the game is released.
